Which AI tool draws the sprite kit best?

Gemini, PixelLab and Retro Diffusion side by side

The same test set each time: a large and a small lily pad, the open flower, the koi and a 2-frame dragonfly, asked for in the chosen style with the kit's own pad, flower and ramps as the reference. Each sheet is pixelified, cut into sprites and snapped to that sprite's ramps. PixelLab's free trial and Retro Diffusion's RD Pro are further down.

What the runs show

The style fits. After snapping, pads and the flower sit on the kit's ramps with almost no colour loss (ΔE 1–4), the outlines are already in the darkest step, and the light comes from the upper left.

The size doesn't. Every 3 Pro Image run drew each sprite 1.7–3.5× too large and ignored every size and layout instruction, including a named canvas. Nano Banana 2.1 came closer (1.1–2.7×) and kept the layout. Shrinking to the target is fine for the pads but wrecks the koi and the dragonfly, so at the kit's sizes those need redrawing by hand, not touching up.

Frames aren't frames. The dragonfly's second frame barely changes pose in any run, and Gemini added, dropped or turned sprites in 4 of 6 runs.

Second try: an exact-size template

Each run got a layout template beside the style reference: a 132 × 56 grid of 24 px cells with one box per sprite at exactly its size in art pixels, and the instruction to paint one art pixel per cell. Nano Banana 2.1 followed it in 1 of 4 runs, and 3 Pro Image in 1 of 3. Even where a sprite landed in its box, the model's pixel grid didn't line up with the cells, and at 18 × 8 and 13 × 9 the koi and dragonfly come back as noise. Only the large pad holds up.

PixelLab, free trial (16 of 40 generations)

Size is solved. PixelLab draws on a canvas of exactly the size asked (16 px at the smallest), so every sprite lands within a few pixels of its target with no shrinking or grid guessing. The palette can be forced on Bitforge, and Pixen's colours snap cleanly.

Quality depends on the model. Seeded with the kit's own pad, Bitforge keeps the shape and adds texture: the best pad on this page. Pixen draws the most readable flower and dragonfly. Text alone on Bitforge, or with the pad as its style image, gives blobs. The koi isn't readable at 18 × 8 or 24 × 12 on any model, and seeding frame 2 from frame 1 kept the pose, so wing frames still need a hand.

Retro Diffusion, RD Pro from code-drawn blockouts ($1.87 of $5)

Exact size, our palette, and the best koi and dragonfly so far. RD Pro draws at exactly the requested size (12 px minimum) and takes a starting image, so the koi and dragonfly start from rough shapes drawn in code. At strength 0.55 the koi reads clearly at 32 × 16. Its own animation mode turned one dragonfly into a real 4-frame wing-beat with the body held still, which nothing else here managed.

Watch the background. RD's background removal takes pale parts (the koi's tail, the dragonfly's wings). Magenta keyed out by us fixes it when the palette isn't forced, but a forced palette repaints the magenta. The small pad came back round, not as the flat oval asked.

How to read a cell

  • AI size, snapped: the sprite at the size Gemini drew it, every pixel moved to the nearest step of its own ramps, shown 3×.
  • Shrunk to target: nearest-neighbour down to the kit size with the outline forced to the darkest step, shown 6× like the kit column. This is what a script gets you before Aseprite.
  • colours before → after snapping; off-ramp is the mean OKLab distance moved (×100; under 3 is invisible); outline is the share of edge pixels already in the ramp's darkest steps.
  • The koi and dragonfly ramps are provisional: the kit never had them.

Runs

  • Run 1: 159x66 art px at 19.8 image px each, 3.7% off-grid. Clean. The dragonfly's second frame differs only in wing colour, not in pose.
  • Run 2: 160x68 art px at 19.6 image px each, 6.5% off-grid. Cleanest pads and flower. The dragonfly frames are almost identical.
  • Run 3: 158x66 art px at 20 image px each, 11.9% off-grid. Drew a third dragonfly. The koi is small and seen side-on.
  • Run 4: 160x67 art px at 19.5 image px each, 14.7% off-grid. Canvas prompt. Drew three koi, two of them identical. Pads are on style.
  • Run 5: 159x67 art px at 19.7 image px each, 11.5% off-grid. Canvas prompt. Both lily pads are drawn standing upright, which is unusable.
  • Run 6: 160x68 art px at 19.65 image px each, 4.4% off-grid. Nano Banana 2.1, the cheap model. It kept the one-row layout and came closest to the sizes, but its edges are softer and it turned the second dragonfly frame 90°.
