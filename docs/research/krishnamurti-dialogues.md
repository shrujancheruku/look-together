# How Krishnamurti's one-to-one dialogues run, turn by turn

Research for [#199](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/199) (map: [#197](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/197)).

**Question:** Read three or four of Krishnamurti's one-to-one dialogues end to end and set down each turn by turn: what the other person brought, what he did at each of his turns, where the exchange stalled, how it ended, and what was left as an Open Question. Keep his words exact where quoted.

## Answer in brief

- **He almost never answers.** Across the four dialogues below (about 170 grouped turns of his), the commonest things he does are to question a word ("Why do you use the word problem?"), to state a view plainly as his own ("All knowledge is time."), to point at a premise packed into what was said, and to leave a question open. A direct answer to the other person's question is rare, and when he gives one it is a refusal of the frame ("A flower has no reason.").
- **He works one thing for a long stretch.** In the Pupul Jayakar dialogue the word "ending" is turned over for most of an hour; in the Saanen discussion a single man's injury and "What am I to do?" is held across the whole morning against a tent trying to change the subject. Within that one thing he does change the question's size often, narrowing ("Is the origin 'I' and 'not I'?") or widening ("Are you including, if I may ask, the whole process of living").
- **He names stalls out loud and in the first person.** "You've led us to an empty wall." "We have spent fifty minutes and we haven't come to any point." "This is going round and round in circles." "This may be just a lot of words." The stall is treated as a fact to look at, not a failure to hide.
- **He slows the other down and himself.** "Wait, wait, wait, wait. Slowly." "Go step by step, please." "You see, we are entering into a very delicate thing."
- **He takes the other person's seat.** With Bohm he speaks as the ordinary person ("Help me; not words; how would you help me to come to that?", "then what is a man to do?"); with Pupul Jayakar he puts himself in the one who clings ("I never find out what it means to end").
- **Endings are by the clock, not by a conclusion.** All four stop with something open and with his saying so: "We'll do it tomorrow.", "What is love, what is death, what is meditation, what is the whole movement of living.", "Shall we stop? Right, it's over.", "let's do it properly: that you are really serious… you, not me." A Recap in our sense (what was looked at) never happens; the closest is one line of his that sums the whole ("Action based on idea is inaction.").
- **He pronounces verdicts and holds his words.** He refuses the other's word ("I wouldn't call that consciousness."), keeps his own under pressure ("I am sticking to that word."), and states conclusions. The not-an-authority stance lives alongside this, not instead of it: "I am not saying you must accept it."

Method, said plainly. The Krishnamurti Foundation Trust pages on kfoundation.org served in full. The reading went through a fetch tool: a first pass over each whole page produced the turn-by-turn record below, and a second pass over the same page checked every quoted line against the page text and returned the full sentence, so every quotation here is a sentence the page holds, with its speaker label. Lines the second pass could not confirm are paraphrased without quotation marks. Direct download of the pages (curl) was not permitted in this run, so no transcript was saved to disk. `jkrishnamurti.org` returned **403 Forbidden** to the fetch tool for `https://jkrishnamurti.org/content/roots-psychological-conflict` (the same Dialogue 1); the kfoundation.org copy was used instead. If the transcripts are to be kept by hand, the five URLs above are the pages.

Speaker labels: K is Krishnamurti, DB is David Bohm, A is Allan Anderson, PJ is Pupul Jayakar, Q is a questioner from the tent. "Turn" below is a transcript turn, and consecutive brief turns are grouped into one row where the record says so. The "his move" column uses this fixed set: `word` (question the word), `premise` (expose a premise or conclusion packed into what was said), `hand back` (the other person does the looking), `fact/idea` (separate the fact from the idea about it), `slow` (slow down, set a precondition), `stall` (name that the exchange is stuck or has drifted), `analogy`, `view` (state a view as his own), `silence`, `open` (leave it as a question), `narrow` (make the question smaller or concrete), `widen`, `refuse frame` (decline the question's terms), `other's seat` (speak as the other person), `return` (pull back to the line), `defer`, `concede`, `close`.

## S1. Dialogue 1 with David Bohm, Ojai, 1 April 1980

**What was brought.** Krishnamurti brings the Problem himself: "Sir, I'd like to ask if humanity has taken a wrong turn." Bohm agrees it happened long ago and from then on plays the part the other person usually plays with K: he asks for the root, doubts a step, and says when he can't follow. Nothing of either man's own life is on the table; the subject is "becoming", time and the self.

## S2. Dialogue 10 with Allan W. Anderson, San Diego, 22 February 1974

**What was brought.** Anderson proposes the subject: seeing, and whether the transformation of a person is independent of knowledge and time. Twice he brings something of his own: the taping of this series and how not to split his attention between the production and the Dialogue (row 14), and a classroom episode from his teaching (row 43). He also offers meditation as the next subject and is told it is too complicated for now.

## S3. Krishnamurti with Pupul Jayakar 4, Brockwood Park, 7 June 1981

**What was brought.** Jayakar brings death: "Krishnaji, one of the questions which I feel lie at the very depth of the human mind…", birth and death as the two poles of life. It turns personal at row 10, where the anguish she means is at Krishnamurti's own ending, and stays personal: whether his consciousness ends with his body, whether she cuts herself off from him when she leaves tomorrow. Of the four, this is the one where the other person's own life is most on the table.

## S4. Public Discussion 1, Saanen, 25 July 1979

**What was brought.** A tent full of questions, and one man's: he "was struck by an automobile when I was seventeen years old", "my leg was torn out", the energy has been blocked since, and he asks about "mental cripples" and, later, "What am I to do?" K works that one question across the morning while the tent keeps pulling toward action, belief, authority and whether the format works at all. This is the one where the other person's life is most plainly on the table and where the stalls are loudest.

Things the counts show that the method note from [#2](krishnamurti-method.md) did not, because it worked from excerpts:

- **"State a view" is his commonest move**, by a wide margin, in the one-to-one settings; with the tent it nearly vanishes and "hand back" and "stall" take over. The excerpt-based note had him "rarely answering"; that is true of answers, not of views.
- **"Question the word" is his opening move three times out of four**, and it recurs through the Jayakar dialogue at a rate of one row in seven. With Bohm it never appears: Bohm's words were already his.
- **Stalls are named in the first person plural** ("we have spent fifty minutes", "we are entering into a very delicate thing", "this is going round and round in circles") and are followed by one of three things: a slowing down, a narrowing to one concrete thing, or an offer to stop.
- **Silence is recorded only with Jayakar**, three pauses, each after a line of his that she could not accept or that summed the line. No pause is recorded in the Bohm or Anderson transcripts.
- **He changes the question's size often and in both directions**, which the five angles (`name | narrow | slow | analogy | reveal`) do not have a word for in the widening direction.
- **He concedes and reverses.** "Of course not. It is not." (S3 row 21), "Quite right." (S2 row 47), "that's all I am saying" (S3 row 52): six rows where the other person's push changes or ends his line.
- **Endings are the clock's.** No Dialogue ends because the question was finished; all four end with a named Open Question and a promise, or a condition, for tomorrow.
- **From S5, not counted:** the clearest case of taking the other's seat ("I'm an ordinary man with all my animal instincts: pleasure and pain and reward and punishment and so on.", "Everything is against me: my family, my wife, my society, everything.", "Then how am I to have it in my daily life?"), the clearest naming of his own loop ("I'm always asking the same question because I'm caught in the same pattern.", "Is there a different approach to this problem?"), and the habitual close ("We always stop here (laughs).").

## What this is raw material for

This note decides nothing. It is the input to the replay prototype in [#201](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/201), which labels each of these turns with our event vocabulary (a Probe, one of the five angles, a reveal, a reword, a reject, a `done` with or without an Open Question, a `propose_add`, an `offer_stop`, or no event we have) and counts what falls through. The four tables are laid out so that each row can be given one label. Things the replay will have to decide, not this note: whether `view` and `concede` have any event; whether `widen`, `other's seat`, `return` and `silence` are angles, events, or gaps; what the 3-angle cap does to the Jayakar dialogue's nine rows on the one word "ending"; and whether "one offer open at a time" survives a tent of forty questions. Checking the Inquiry rules themselves against these dialogues is the map's job ([#197](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/197)), not this ticket's.
