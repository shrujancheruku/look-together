# What Krishnamurti held inquiry to be, and what he ruled out of it

Research for [#198](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/198) (map: [#197](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/197)). Builds on [`krishnamurti-method.md`](krishnamurti-method.md) (#2), which catalogued his *moves* from short excerpts. This doc reads whole texts and asks what he held inquiry *to be* and what he explicitly ruled out. It does not repeat the moves; where a move matters it points at the method doc. Sources were read on 2026-11-08.

**Question:** In his own texts, read end to end, what did Krishnamurti hold inquiry to be and what did he rule out? For each principle: one line in his terms, a dated source, and any place his practice in dialogue departed from it. Then: which Inquiry rules does each principle bear on, as a fit or a tension, deciding nothing.

## Answer in brief

- **Inquiry, for him, was two people "together" looking at one question until "only the question remains and not the persons"** (Ojai 1984). Its ground is "pure observation without direction" (Core, 1980), and its only material is the fact of what one is now, "not what you would like to be" (FLF, 1954).
- **He ruled out, by name:** any "psychological technique", "method" or "system" (Core; FLF); "intellectual analysis or introspective dissection" (Core), because "the analyser becomes the censor" and analysis "bit by bit" has no end (IQ, 1972; AI, 1973); authority of every kind, including his own ("doubt what they are saying, including the speaker", Saanen 1980); seeking an answer, a solution or a result ("if you have a motive, it has already set a direction", Saanen 1984); the word, the opposite and the ideal as ways of looking ("The non-violence is non-fact", Brockwood 1983); comparison and "the more", which "is a measurement" (Ojai 1984); and the division of observer from observed ("the observer is the observed", FFK, 1969).
- **What he held in place of these:** put the right question and stay in it; look at the fact without the word; "remain with it without any movement away from it" (Saanen 1980); let perception act ("when you see clearly the problem is solved", FFK). Understanding, when it comes, is "immediate", not the end of steps (Saanen 1980; FLF).
- **His practice departed from his principles in three recurring ways,** all of them differences of *how* rather than of what he ruled out: he asserted facts and verdicts freely ("That is a fact. Right?"; "you are very lost people"); he answered for the room when it did not answer ("I'll reply for you"); and he set questions aside as wrong or off the point ("You have put a wrong question, then you answer it wrongly"). He also taught one explanation as a sequence (seeing, contact, sensation, thought, desire) in meeting after meeting, which is close to the "step by step" he otherwise refused.
- **For the Inquiry,** the fits are strong where the app refuses to conclude, keeps the Problem as something to face, hands the question back and shows "what was looked at, not what was learned". The tensions worth taking to the map are: the Inquiry *is* a fixed structure with counted angles (his "system"); the Breakdown draws premises out of the person's words (his "analysis", though he did exactly this himself); outcomes and a "worth your time" question are measures; and the Open Question is a fallback where for him it was the point. Details in [Mapping onto the Inquiry](#mapping-onto-the-inquiry-rules).

## Principles, in his terms

Each section gives his principle in one line, what he said, and where his practice in the meetings departed from it. "Departed" is read against the test in #197: only an authority claimed, a system to follow, a result promised, or a naming, condemning or measuring of what is looked at counts against the principle; bluntness and length are logged, not judged.

### 1. No method, system or technique

**Principle.** Truth is "a pathless land"; there is no method of self-knowledge, because following a method produces the result the method was built for, not understanding.

- "Man cannot come to it through any organisation, through any creed, through any dogma, priest or ritual, nor through any philosophical knowledge or psychological technique." (C, 1980)
- "Therefore there is no method for self-knowledge. Seeking a method invariably implies the desire to attain some result." And: "the following of a system will merely produce a result created by that system." (FLF-method, 1954)
- "It isn't something that you go step by step by step, climb the ladder." "The understanding of *what is* is immediate." (S80)
- "The first step is the first step." When asked what the first step is, the answer is "Stop." then "turn south". (S80)
- "it is not little by little by little, first step, second step, third step, first initiation, second initiation, but to see the truth of this instantly" (S80)
- Awareness "cannot be practised. When you practise a thing, it becomes a habit and awareness is not habit." (FLF-intro)
- "Learning is discipline, not conformity." (O84b) He kept a sense of discipline that is "its own order": "the attention to this whole movement is its own discipline" (B1).

**Departures.** In every meeting read he gave the same account of desire as a sequence to be slowed "like in a film, step by step": "seeing, contact, sensation. Then thought … at that moment desire is born" (B1; also S80, O84b). He presented this as fact ("Is this a fact? … This is a fact."), not as a technique, but it is a repeatable procedure he taught across years. He also gave direction in ordered form: "first … then" ("can we look at all that without reaction first? Then find out if we can look at all that without the network of words", S84).

### 2. Not analysis, not introspection: observation

**Principle.** Self-knowledge comes "through observation and not through intellectual analysis or introspective dissection"; analysis is partial, takes time and sets the analyser apart from what is analysed.

- Core, 1980, as quoted above.
- "The more we think over a problem, the more we investigate, analyse and discuss it, the more complex it becomes." "Can we understand this self, not by analysing, but by seeing the thing as it is, being aware of it as a fact and not as a theory?" (FLF-think)
- Introspection "in order to improve, in order to change" has "always an end in view; when that end is not achieved, there is moodiness, depression." Awareness, by contrast, "is observation without condemnation" and has "no end in view". (FLF-intro)
- "I do not think anything is going to be achieved through analysis, either by ourselves or by a professional." But: "I must know how to analyse which means that in the process of analysis my mind becomes extraordinarily sharp … One hasn't the eyes to see the whole thing at a glance; this clarity of the eye is possible only if one can see the details, then jump." (FFK-anger, 1969)
- The fullest statement: "Analysis implies division. There is the analyser and that which is to be analysed. Whether you analyse yourself, or it is done by a specialist, there is division, therefore there is already the beginning of conflict." "Of the many fragments into which we are divided, one assumes the authority of the analyser … That analyser becomes the censor; he, with his accumulated knowledge, evaluates the good and the bad". "You can spend all your days analysing and at the end of it you are where you were, with still more to be analysed." The alternative is not emotion but perception: "Either you enquire analytically or you perceive immediately the root of it." "It is like looking at the total extent of the map, not the detail of where you want to go." (IQ-analysis, 1972)
- "To the speaker, analysis is the denial of action; action being always in the active present." "if it is to be examined bit by bit, one by one, you are entering into the world of analysis and there is no end to that". He allowed one exception: "maybe it is necessary for those who are somewhat, or greatly, unbalanced; and perhaps most of us are unbalanced." And he set his own meetings apart from it: "This is not group therapy! … it is not that the speaker is analysing you, or making you change". "The moment you try to correct it, you are bringing disorder. But if you merely observe actually what is, then what is, is order." (AI-analysis, 1973)
- "It requires a very attentive, not analytical, but observing brain to see where the subtleties of the self are hidden." (S84)
- "You don't have to analyse, go through all the tortures… it all becomes so childish." (S80)
- Asked "Does it not need conscious analysis to arrive at that conclusion?", he answered: "Is it a conclusion, resulting from analysis? You just observe this fact. … you can see it – is that analysis?" (IQ-analysis)
- He placed the professional analyst beside the guru and the oracle as people one goes to instead of looking: "the analyst and the analyser, and find out the answer from another" (O83); "you went to the psychologist with your problems, or to the priest to confess" (S80).

**Departures.** He dissected questions constantly and in public: the "understand" in "I understand that all people have a similar consciousness" (O84a), the conclusion packed into "What is the action of not letting memory intrude?" ("you have already come to that conclusion", S84), the premise in "How can one reconcile the demands of society" ("you are society", S84). That is analysis of the *question*, done on the spot, with no end in view beyond opening it; his objection was to analysis of *oneself over time aimed at change*. The distinction is his, and the FFK "see the details, then jump" line shows he kept a place for taking things apart.

### 3. No authority, no guide: the mirror and the two friends

**Principle.** Nobody can teach you about yourself; the speaker is not answering, "we are together investigating", and when you see yourself clearly "you can break the mirror, and the man who holds up the mirror".

- "we are together investigating the question, which becomes much more fascinating … and therefore there is no authority" (S80)
- "the first thing, sir, whether they are eastern gurus or western gurus, is to doubt what they are saying, including the speaker; much more so, because I'm very clear about all these matters." (S80)
- "there is no right guru; there is only the wrong guru. Because nobody can teach you anything except for yourself." "you are the teacher and the disciple; there is no teacher or a disciple outside you." The word guru, he said, means "one who points. Points — not the way, not… just points." (O83)
- "when you listen to K he is not instructing you. He's putting up a mirror in front of you to see yourself. … And when you see yourself very clearly you can break the mirror, and the man who holds up the mirror." (B2)
- "this is a dialogue between us; not a monologue by me … Neither is trying to impress the other or convince the other or subtly persuading the other, but two friends talking over together." (B2)
- On being asked how he knows what he says is true: "it is not what I say to be true, but the fact itself. Facts themselves show what the truth is." (B1)
- "Authority prevents the understanding of oneself … Under the shelter of an authority, a guide, you may have temporarily a sense of security". "Another can point out the way but you have to do all the work." (FLF-method; FLF-guru)
- He designated no successor: "he has designated no-one, no teacher, or anyone to represent after he has gone" (O84a).
- Dependence on anything outside, however fine, is "another form of drug": "you have to be a light to yourself. And you cannot depend on the light of others." (B2)

**Departures.** This is where the gap between principle and practice is widest, and it is the gap the method doc already noted. He used his own authority to settle things: "I am an Indian – that is an illusion. … you are British – that is also an illusion." "with your faiths, with your beliefs, ideals you are already lost. That is a fact. You are very lost people." (B1) "It's such an absurd question. … Sorry, whoever put that question." (S84) "if you will forgive me … you really don't understand what you have said." (S84) He used etymology as a court of appeal in nearly every answer (problem, idea, art, guru, mantra, austere, question, individual, approach, opinion), and he said "I'm very clear about all these matters" in the same breath as "doubt … the speaker". When the room would not answer he answered for it: "there are too many people, therefore I'll reply for you" (O84a). He also refused the charge: "It's not a question of I trusting you or you trusting me", "I'm not hypnotising you" (B2, S84), and "You're not learning anything from me, please; I'm just pointing out" (B2). His own frame for all this was the mirror: a mirror can be blunt and still not be an authority. Whether the Inquiry can use that frame is a question for the map, not for this doc.

### 4. The question over the answer; the answer is in the problem

**Principle.** A question is a problem thrown at you; the right question, approached without motive, carries its own answer, and the aim of a dialogue is that "only the question remains".

- "The word *question* comes from Latin, *to seek*. And when one puts a question one is actually seeking the answer in the question itself." "If we know how to put the right question, we'll find invariably for ourselves the right answer. And it is very difficult to put a right question; it implies that one has thought a great deal about the problem and followed the problem in sequence". (S80)
- "Is the question more important than the answer? Does the answer lie in the question?" "it's important also to put the right question." Superficial questions ("What kind of food should I eat?") are set aside; a deep question asks "why one puts the question". (O83)
- The dialogue form, stated once in full: "you ask a question and the speaker replies to that question. Then you respond to that question … and so keep this going till only the question remains and not the persons. … So the question then has tremendous vitality; it's not tinged by any personal prejudice." (O84a)
- "We're going to examine the questions, not the answers." "the importance is in the question, not in the answer." (O84a)
- Approach: "The word *approach* means coming very near … Shouldn't we approach the problem tentatively, hesitantly, and if you have a motive then the motive directs the problem. … If we have any kind of direction, which is the solution, then we have already limited it." "so lets both of us approach it without motive, without any kind of reaction, like or dislike, but just listen to the question first". (S84)
- Wrong questions exist and are named: "You have put a wrong question, then you answer it wrongly." (S84) "Moving from here to there. That's a wrong question obviously. Because if you move from here to that, that is the same as this." (S84) "Would it be a right question to ask: what effect will it have? … I think that's a wrong approach to the question." (S84) "Who is exploring? … Surely such a question is a wrong question, is it not?" (FLF-intro)
- Problems are not to be solved by a brain full of problems: "unless it is free from problems in itself, it cannot possibly solve the problems without raising other problems." (B2) "Questions imply problems and problems are to be solved. Not in the resolution of one problem a dozen other problems arise. The ending of a problem, of any problem." (O84b)
- Patience, not speed: "we want quick — all of us do … we take a pill quickly, but the cause is there." "Patience is not time. Patience is the quality of a brain that is looking, watching." (S84) "You must begin near to go far". (FLF-intro)
- Words that close a question: "When I use the word *impossible*, *difficult*, 'I am a failure', those words act as a barrier." "'I will try' the speaker says don't try. The moment you try you're making an effort, and when you make an effort you don't understand anything." (O84a) "If you say either of those things you are blocking yourself. … If you say, 'I can't walk up that mountain', you've stopped walking." (S84)

**Departures.** He answered. In O84a he said "We'll answer this question. We'll stop this back and forth" after two questions and gave long expositions on thought and memory to the end. He chose which questions were worth asking and said so ("a lot of theoretical, ideological questions that really have no meaning at all", O84b), and he declined tangents: "Don't go back to babies and children and mothers, and all that. That is a different question." (B1) He let a questioner hijack with a question about marriage ("All right, sir. Let's have some fun", O83) and came back to the question he had been on afterward. Also, "wrong question" is itself a verdict on the person's question; he used it often.

### 5. Observation without the word, without condemning or justifying

**Principle.** To look is to look without the word that names the feeling and without the reaction that condemns, justifies or explains it; the name "gives it continuity, gives it strength".

- "Freedom is pure observation without direction, without fear of punishment and reward." "Freedom is found in the choiceless awareness of our daily existence." (C, 1980)
- "do we observe anything without the word? Do we observe the mountain and not call it mountain?" "When you say, 'He's a communist or a totalitarian' you have wiped it out … you have put him in a category, in a cage and that's the end of it." (S84)
- "I use the word *greed* to identify that sensation. … Moment that recognition takes place I've already strengthened that reaction because I recognise it and take it back into my memory." (S80)
- "The moment you give a name to that which you call fear, you strengthen it; but if you can look at that feeling without terming it, you will see that it withers away." (FLF-fear)
- "to watch it without any condemnation, justification or explanation, just to watch it." (B1) "So can you look at anger without any explanation or justification … Can you look at it completely objectively, which means neither defending it nor condemning it?" (FFK-anger)
- "If I want to understand somebody, I cannot condemn him: I must observe, study him. I must love the very thing I am studying." (FLF-method)
- "'know yourself' … means watch what you are doing … Not correct; not say this is right or wrong, just watch." (O83)
- "I'm not an oracle." "We don't condemn it or approve it or disapprove it. These are facts". (S80; O84b, on homosexuality)
- "that observation itself is silence." "If you're watching the speaker and you have reactions, you're not watching him; you're watching your reactions." (S84)
- On recording: "When there is complete attention there is no recording." Psychological recording "gives strength, builds up the ego". (S84)

**Departures.** He named and condemned freely in the room: "nonsense", "rubbish", "the stupidity of religions", "this tribal insular worship", "some kind of idiot", "barbarians". He described people's states for them ("You are exceptionally silent", "Don't look so paralysed", S84). None of this was turned on what the *questioner* was looking at in themselves, which is what the principle protects; it was turned on institutions, ideas and the room's inattention. He did, though, name people's feelings about his words: "I know you will not like this but that's a fact." (B2)

### 6. No measurement, no comparison, no becoming

**Principle.** Comparison is a form of violence; "the more" is measurement and measurement is limited; the effort to become is "a direct avoidance of what is".

- "When I compare myself with you … Through comparison I make myself dull." "this comparison is a form of violence. … So imitation is violence." (B1)
- "Knowledge can expand, more and more … When there is more, there is still more. … More is a measurement. … Whenever I say, 'I am better', it is limited." (O84a)
- "Thinking about oneself, which we all do: 'I am making progress; I am better than yesterday; I have my problems' … it's really going round and round in circles." Observing oneself "is something entirely different." (S84)
- "Being virtuous comes through the understanding of what is, whereas becoming virtuous is postponement, the covering up of what is with what you would like to be." (FLF-method)
- "Effort is a distraction from what is. … becoming is a direct avoidance of what is." (FLF-become)
- "Transformation is not an end, a result. … that which is becoming can never know that which is being." (FLF-false)
- "the meaning of the word *mantra* means ponder over not becoming … dissolve the whole structure of becoming. So it means there is no evolution for the self, for the psyche." (O83)
- Reward and punishment: "reward and punishment they are the two elements we live on." Can a habit end "without any action of will, decision, compulsion, reward"? (B1)
- Choice itself is measured against: "where there is choice there is a conflict." (O84a)

**Departures.** He measured the room's seriousness constantly ("Verbally we'll go", "Intellectually you see the reason … but to feel it", B2; "Are you working the same as the speaker is working, or you're just listening?", O84b) and ranked questions by depth. He also compared peoples ("the Christians have killed more than anybody else … the Buddhists and the Hindus come on a lower scale", B1). These are comparisons *he* made, not ones he asked the questioner to make of themselves.

### 7. "What is", not "what should be"

**Principle.** The ideal is "non-fact"; begin where you are ("Truth is I am angry, I am jealous"), deal with the fact only and never its opposite.

- "Truth is not something that is mysterious, truth is where you are. … Truth is I am angry, I am jealous, I am aggressive, I quarrel. That is a fact. So one must begin … where one is." (B1)
- "when I say, 'I am violent', there is immediately an idea I must not be violent. … The non-violence is non-fact." "Let's find out how to deal with the fact only, not with its opposite." (B1)
- "The 'what is' is what you are, not what you would like to be; it is not the ideal, because the ideal is fictitious". (FLF-method)
- "you are avoiding the fact when you make an ideal of the fact." "if one can look at the fact and not make an idea of it … but remain with the fact". (O84a)
- Fact and idea: "What we do is see a fact and make an abstraction of it and then pursue the idea." (B1) "Thinking about a fact is something totally different from the fact." (O84a)
- Fact and opinion: "Fact is that which has happened … and fact is not what will happen." "A fact can be made into an opinion, but the opinion is not the fact." Having opinions "seems to one such a stupid waste of energy". (O84b)
- Facts can be illusions and still facts: "If I say I am a Hindu and I stick to it, that is a fact. Whether it is an illusion … that also is fact. … Fact can be an illusion, or actual." (B1)
- "Are we afraid of a fact or of an idea about the fact?" (FLF-fear)
- Negation first: "through negation you come to the positive. But if you start with the positive, you end up with negation." (O83) "Total negation is the essence of the positive." (C)

**Departures.** His own "what should be" is visible in the subjunctives he used for the world ("we'd put an end to wars"; "There can never be united Europe", B2) and in his verdict that most people "live in a make-believe world" (O84b). Within an exchange he held to it: "I am not saying you should or should not have ideals. I am saying, why do we have ideals". (B1)

### 8. The observer is the observed

**Principle.** The one who watches a feeling is made of the same memory as the feeling; the division is an illusion, and seeing that ends the conflict because "if something is you, what can you do?".

- "he will see the division between the thinker and the thought, the observer and the observed, the experiencer and the experience. He will discover that this division is an illusion. Then only is there pure observation which is insight without any shadow of the past." (C, 1980)
- "the observer is the result of all the other images – therefore the observer is the observed." "For what is he to do? If something is you, what can you do? You cannot rebel against it or run away from it or even accept it. It is there." (FFK-observer, 1969)
- "If you ask yourself a question, who is the entity who is going to receive the answer? And who is the entity who is going to enquire?" (FFK-observer)
- "The observer is thought, and that which he's observing as thought is still thought. … So two thoughts, one thought is watching the other thought." (O84a)
- "The watcher is not different from chattering. So the watcher is not the structure of words, memories, he is just watching." (B1)
- "At the moment of experience, there is neither the observer nor the observed: there is only the experiencing. … The moment, the second afterwards, you term it, you name it … so you have created immediately the observer and the observed". (FLF-intro)
- "thought has created the thinker … Has not thought created the thinker?" and when told "I can see it logically": "Wait, sir; first of all, let's be logical." then "Intellectual perception is partial sensitivity". (O83)
- "This awareness that the observer is the observed is not a process of identification with the observed." (FFK-observer)
- "division between the observer and the thing observed: this is the root cause of conflict." (IQ-analysis) "As long as I say, 'Anger is different from me, I must control anger, I must change, I must control my thoughts', in that there is division, therefore there is conflict." "If you really see the beauty of this, that the observer is the observed … then you can observe the totality of consciousness without analysis." (AI-analysis)

**Departures.** None of substance in the meetings; he treated it as the hardest thing and said so ("Go slowly, go very slowly, because it is a very complex thing"). Where the room claimed to see it he doubted them, which is the authority point again (B2: "You haven't proved it yet though, have you?" "I seem to be probing; you are not probing.").

### 9. Ending, conclusions and staying with a question

**Principle.** A stated conclusion ends inquiry; the fact must be stayed with "without any movement away from it"; and understanding, when it comes, is complete and leaves nothing to carry ("What you understand leaves no mark").

- "See the fact and remain with the fact – you understand? – remain with it without any movement away from it. That is the depth of austerity." "to see it completely, don't move… thought mustn't interfere. … Just observe it. And you will see, if you do, real depth of understanding comes". (S80)
- "We never proceed to find out anything to its very end. I don't know where the end is but we'll go on till we discover something." (O84a) "Do you ever stick to one thing and pursue it to the very end?" (S84) "we never pursue the question to the end. We may pursue it intellectually, verbally, but to do it with your heart". (O84b)
- Conclusions: "there is always the fact and a conclusion from the fact … pursue the conclusion, not the understanding of the fact." (B1) "let's look into it; don't assert. You understand? Don't take a position, then … it becomes a battle." "taking up any position, that, 'I believe in this', then you can't discuss, you can't explore." (O84a) "these opinions, conclusions, concepts, ideals divide human beings." (S84)
- "When you start with certainty you end up in uncertainty." (S80)
- Ending as the result of seeing, not of effort: "When you see clearly the problem is solved." "if you say, 'I must not hate; I must have love in my heart', then you are living in a hypocritical world". (FFK-anger) "The realisation of a truth, that very realisation brings a radical change." (O84a) "that very perception burns away the demand". (O84b)
- No carrying over: "What you understand leaves no mark; therefore understanding or truth is the liberator." "Understanding is non-continuous, it is from moment to moment, unresidual." (FLF-now) "the memory of that clarity is not the fact of clarity." (O84b)
- No overcoming: "anything that is overcome has to be conquered again and again. No problem can be finally overcome, conquered; it can be understood but not conquered." (FLF-fear)
- Not knowing kept open: "I don't know where the end is". On the role of art: "I don't know. But we're going to find out together." (O83) The three states of answering: "instant reply; taking time to respond …; and the other is to say, 'I really don't know.'" (O84a)
- Truth heard and not acted on "acts as poison": "It's much better not to hear the truth, then you can carry on in your old way." (O84a)

**Departures.** He closed meetings on time rather than at an end ("Is that enough for today? What time is it?", B1; "May I get up, please?", S84), and he moved on when the room could not follow ("Oh, I see you can't deal with this; you are… All right, sir, let's go to our questions", B2), which is a question *left*, not stayed with. He also drew conclusions and stated them as such: "So we are saying there is no individual consciousness"; "we are humanity" (O84a). On his own account these were facts to be seen, not conclusions; the room was not always able to tell the difference, and he knew it ("either you accept it as an idea, or … make an abstraction of it and say it's a good ideal", O84a).

## Two things the map asked about and the texts answer

**Turns that are not questions.** His turns were very often not questions but imperatives to look: "Look." "Just watch it." "Go slowly." "Don't assert." "Push it. Push it deeply." "Do it please, try it once." "Don't look so paralysed." (B1, S84, O84a) Alongside these he used "Right?" and "You understand?" as a continuous check for assent, not as questions to sit with. So the texts give evidence for the "plain observation" turn the map has as "not yet specified", and for the check-in that follows one.

**The word as the Probe.** He opened almost every answer by taking one word out of the question and asking what it means: *understand* (O84a, S84), *consciousness*, *interest*, *individual* (O84a), *art*, *guru*, *mantra* (O83), *know*, *austere*, *integrity* (S80), *approach*, *difficult* (S84), *fact*, *idea* (B1), *opinion*, *judgement*, *law*, *justice* (O84b, B2). The word came first and the dictionary or Sanskrit root second; the person's own meaning was asked for ("What do you mean by understanding?") but then supplied by him. This bears on whether the key word should be the whole Probe.

## Mapping onto the Inquiry rules

This section names fits and tensions against the Inquiry section of [`current-state.md`](../current-state.md), the glossary and ADRs [0001](../adr/0001-never-judge-assumptions.md) and [0004](../adr/0004-inquiry-is-an-app-owned-state-machine.md). It decides nothing. "Contradiction" below uses #197's test (authority, system to be followed, promised result, or naming/condemning/measuring what is looked at); everything else is a difference of how he worked.

### Questions this raises for the map, not answered here

1. Is the angle set a "psychological technique" in his sense (something the person practises toward a result) or an authoring discipline the person never meets? The answer decides whether principle 1 is a contradiction or a difference of how.
2. Does the `name` angle name a feeling, a word or a premise? Principle 5 falls differently on each.
3. Should "only the question remains" be the default end of a Path, with outcomes recorded quietly, rather than the Open Question being a fallback (principle 4)?
4. Is "Was this worth your time?" a measure the person is invited to make of the Inquiry's result (principle 6)?
5. Can a turn be an imperative to look ("Look at that word") rather than a question, as his often were, and still keep "one question per turn"?
