# Platform rules for an ambient soundscape and sound effects in Expo (iOS, Android, web)

Research for [#204](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/204), on the [pixel-world map](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/196). Sources were read on 2026-11-08 against **Expo SDK 58** (`expo-audio` 58.x, read on the `sdk-58` branch). This note says what the platforms impose and what `expo-audio` lets us do; it does **not** decide whether the world has sound. That is [#212](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/212).

What we already use ([Expo libraries][45], the sheet's Voice section): `expo-audio` streaming for dictation on native and our own `getUserMedia` + `AudioWorklet` on web; `POST /speak` returns Cloud TTS audio that the device plays on a "listen" tap; starting dictation stops playback. A soundscape would share one `AVAudioSession` (iOS), one audio-focus holder (Android) and, on web, whatever `AudioContext` we create with those.

Claims marked **(unverified)** come from secondary sources, from reading code rather than docs, or from reasoning; everything else is quoted or paraphrased from the primary source linked beside it.

## Answer in short

- **iOS decides the silent switch by audio session category, and `expo-audio` picks the category from one flag.** `playsInSilentMode: false` gives `.ambient` (mixes with other apps, silenced by the Ring/Silent switch and by screen lock); `true` gives `.playback` (ignores the switch, and `mixWithOthers` / `duckOthers` become available). Apple's HIG says a device in silent mode "plays only the audio that people explicitly initiate, like media playback", and names "game soundtracks" among the sounds to silence. A soundscape belongs in `.ambient`; "listen" is explicitly initiated. The session is one per app, so the sound module has to switch modes at the tap, or accept that "listen" also obeys the switch.
- **Android has no silent switch for media.** Ringer modes and Do Not Disturb leave media alone unless the person chooses otherwise. What Android has is **audio focus**: `expo-audio`'s `mixWithOthers` requests none (so no call or focus-loss callbacks), `doNotMix` requests transient exclusive focus, `duckOthers` requests `TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK`. Android and Apple both say ducking others is for short, intermittent audio, so a 20-minute soundscape must not duck other apps' music: it mixes or it takes focus.
- **Web needs a gesture, and iPhone Safari behaves like `.ambient`.** No browser lets audio start before the person has interacted with the page; an `AudioContext` created earlier sits `suspended`. Audio keeps a hidden tab unthrottled on desktop, while iOS Safari sets the context to `interrupted` when the page is left. On iPhone, Web Audio output is muted by the ringer switch while `<audio>` elements are not **(unverified: WebKit bug tracker and developer reports)**. `expo-audio` on web is an `HTMLAudioElement` wrapper: `loop` is the element's `loop`, `volume` is **not settable on iOS Safari**, `setAudioModeAsync` is a no-op, and there is no gain ramp. Our own Web Audio player (we already own an `AudioWorklet`) gets sample-accurate looping and gain ramps for free.
- **Battery:** Android's own guidance is that "for short audio playbacks or playbacks when the screen is on, audio does not have a significant impact on power". Our soundscape is foreground-only and screen-on, so the decoder is not the battery story; the Skia world is. The avoidable costs are `expo-audio`'s status polling (`updateInterval` 500 ms by default, a JS event twice a second) and audio sampling (which on Android needs `RECORD_AUDIO`; never enable it).
- **Gapless and crossfade:** `expo-audio`'s single-player `loop` enqueues a copy of the item on an `AVQueuePlayer` (iOS) and sets `REPEAT_MODE_ONE` on ExoPlayer (Android); neither is documented as gapless and the playlist's `loop: 'single'` on iOS is a seek-to-zero, so use the player's `loop`, not a playlist. There is **no fade or crossfade API** on any platform; volume is set instantly. Web Audio's `AudioBufferSourceNode.loop` has "subsample accuracy" by spec, and `GainNode` ramps give fades.
- **Ducking under "listen" is our own arithmetic** (both players are ours), stopping for dictation is forced on iOS anyway: the stream sets the session to `.record`, which "silenc[es] playback audio", and never restores it, so the sound module must pause before `stream.start()` and re-apply the mode after `stream.stop()`. On Android and web the stream doesn't touch the soundscape, so pausing is on us, and the microphone would otherwise hear the speaker.
- **Accessibility:** WCAG 2.2 **1.4.2 Audio Control (Level A)** applies to any audio that "plays automatically for more than 3 seconds": there must be a way to pause or stop it, or a volume control independent of the system's, and "muting the system volume is not pausing or stopping". It covers the whole page and, through WCAG2ICT, the whole app. Cue sounds under 3 s fall outside it; the soundscape does not. Apple's HIG adds "avoid autoplaying audio … without also providing controls to start and stop it" and "consider global settings that let people opt out". VoiceOver's and TalkBack's audio ducking lower media while they speak (TalkBack's is on by default), Mono Audio sums the channels, and hearing devices receive media through the system's own routing with nothing for the app to do.

## 1. iOS: the silent switch, categories and mixing

### What the platform imposes

| Category | Silenced by the Ring/Silent switch and screen lock | Interrupts nonmixable apps' audio | I/O |
|---|---|---|---|
| `ambient` | Yes | No (mixes) | Output |
| `soloAmbient` (default) | Yes | Yes | Output |
| `playback` | No | Yes by default; no with `mixWithOthers` | Output |
| `record` | No | Yes | Input only ("silencing playback audio") |
| `playAndRecord` | No | Yes by default; no with `mixWithOthers` | Input and output |

[Audio Session Programming Guide, Table B-1](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/Audio/Conceptual/AudioSessionProgrammingGuide/AudioSessionCategoriesandModes/AudioSessionCategoriesandModes.html) (archived, still Apple's only table), [AVAudioSession.Category](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct), [`.ambient`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/ambient): "When you use this category, audio from other apps mixes with your audio. Screen locking and the Silent switch … silence your audio." [`.playback`](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/category-swift.struct/playback): "your app audio continues with the Silent switch set to silent or when the screen locks."

- **Options are for `playback` only.** `mixWithOthers`, `duckOthers` and `interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers` "can [be] set … only if the audio session category is playAndRecord, playback, or multiRoute"; `ambient` sets `mixWithOthers` automatically. [mixWithOthers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/categoryoptions-swift.struct/mixwithothers)
- **Ducking other apps is for seconds, not sessions.** `duckOthers`: "Set this option on a temporary basis only. Don't use it to duck the audio of other apps for more than a few seconds." Ducking lasts from session activation to deactivation. [duckOthers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/categoryoptions-swift.struct/duckothers)
- **Spoken audio has its own rules.** `interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers` pauses other apps' sessions in `.spokenAudio` mode (podcasts, audiobooks) while ours is active, and Apple says to pair it with `duckOthers` for "occasional and spoken" audio such as navigation prompts. [interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/categoryoptions-swift.struct/interruptspokenaudioandmixwithothers), [Mode.spokenAudio](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/mode-swift.struct/spokenaudio). This is the shape "listen" has, and the shape a soundscape does not.
- **The HIG's rule for silence:** "People switch a device to silent when they want to avoid being interrupted by unexpected sounds … they also want to silence nonessential sounds, such as keyboard clicks, sound effects, game soundtracks, and other audible feedback. When a device is in silent mode, it plays only the audio that people explicitly initiate, like media playback, alarms, and audio/video messaging." And: "don't make people stop listening to music from another app if you don't need to." Its category table puts "a game that lets people play music from another app during gameplay in place of the game's soundtrack" under **Ambient**. [HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio)
- **Volume:** "People expect their volume settings to affect all sound in the system … Adjust levels automatically when necessary — don't adjust the overall volume. Your app can adjust relative, independent volume levels … but the system volume always governs the final output." Headphones: "when disconnecting headphones, they expect playback to pause immediately." [HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio)
- **Other apps' primary audio:** Apple offers `secondaryAudioShouldBeSilencedHint` ("in a game that uses the ambient category, you can use this property to mute the soundtrack while leaving sound effects unmuted") and `silenceSecondaryAudioHintNotification`. [secondaryAudioShouldBeSilencedHint](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/secondaryaudioshouldbesilencedhint) This is exactly the soundscape-versus-cue split, and **`expo-audio` does not expose it** (nothing in the SDK 58 reference or `AudioModule.swift`). A few lines of native module if we want it.
- **Interruptions** (a call, an alarm, Siri, another nonmixable session) deactivate the session; on `ended`, `shouldResume` says whether to resume. `AVPlayer` "automatically pauses playback in response to interruption events". iOS 27 adds lifecycle notifications and a `resumptionRecommendationNotification`, and marks the old `InterruptionType`/`InterruptionOptions` deprecated. [Handling audio interruptions](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/handling-audio-interruptions), [interruptionNotification](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfaudio/avaudiosession/interruptionnotification). The HIG: "a game doesn't need to check the interruption type before automatically resuming playback, because a game plays audio without an explicit user choice." [HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio)

### What `expo-audio` does with it

Read from [`AudioModule.swift`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/ios/AudioModule.swift), [`AudioRecords.swift`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/ios/AudioRecords.swift) and the [SDK 58 reference](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/audio/).

- **One global mode**, `setAudioModeAsync(partial)`, maps to the category:
  - `playsInSilentMode: false` → `.soloAmbient` if `interruptionMode` is `doNotMix` / `doNotMixPersistent`, else `.ambient`. **Options are cleared** in this branch, so `duckOthers` is silently ignored under the switch-respecting categories (as Apple requires).
  - `playsInSilentMode: true` → `.playback` (or `.playAndRecord` when `allowsRecording`), with `.mixWithOthers` or `.duckOthers` added for those modes and nothing for `doNotMix*`.
  - `InterruptionMode` is `mixWithOthers` (default) | `doNotMix` | `doNotMixPersistent` | `duckOthers`. `doNotMixPersistent` is the only mode that does not notify others on deactivation.
- **Default discrepancy (unverified which wins at runtime):** the docs say `playsInSilentMode` defaults to `true`, and the Kotlin record agrees, but the Swift record declares `@Field var playsInSilentMode: Bool = false`. A partial `setAudioModeAsync` that omits the flag may therefore land on `.ambient` on iOS. Set it explicitly, per platform.
- **Interruption began:** players are paused in every mode except `duckOthers`, where volume is halved (`volume *= 0.5`) and restored on end; players resume on `ended` only if `options.contains(.shouldResume)`. Route change `.oldDeviceUnavailable` (headphones out) pauses all players; the docs: audio "automatically stops if headphones/Bluetooth audio devices are disconnected." It still uses the legacy `interruptionNotification`, which iOS 27 deprecates but keeps.
- **Session lifetime:** 58.0.0 "activates the audio session once and keeps it active instead of toggling"; `deactivateSession()` waits 100 ms and only deactivates when nothing plays or records and no `keepAudioSessionActive` keeper exists. `setIsAudioActiveAsync(false)` pauses everything and deactivates. [CHANGELOG](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/CHANGELOG.md)
- **Background:** `shouldPlayInBackground` defaults to `false`; the `audio` background mode is added by the config plugin (`enableBackgroundPlayback` defaults to `true`, which we can turn off: a soundscape with no lock-screen controls has no business in the background, and `.ambient` is silenced by the lock anyway).

**What this means for a soundscape on iOS:** `.ambient` is the right home (mixes with the person's own music, obeys the switch and the lock, no ducking of others), which is `playsInSilentMode: false` + `mixWithOthers` in `expo-audio` terms. Because the mode is app-wide, "listen" under the same mode is also silenced by the switch; playing TTS through the switch means flipping to `playsInSilentMode: true` for the duration of the clip and back after `didJustFinish` **(unverified: whether `setCategory` mid-session glitches the running soundscape; test on a device)**. Either way the sound module is the only caller of `setAudioModeAsync`, as the session rule in the sheet already says for the backend `client`.

## 2. Android: audio focus, ducking, ringer and Do Not Disturb

### What the platform imposes

- **Focus is the contract, enforced since Android 12.** "Only one app can hold audio focus at a time." Request it "immediately before starting to play", pause or duck on loss, abandon it when done. On Android 12+ "the system forces audio playback from an app to fade out when another app requests audio focus" (for `USAGE_MEDIA`/`USAGE_GAME`, `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN`, non-speech content) and "mutes audio playback when an incoming call is received" for apps that hold focus and are playing. Apps targeting Android 15+ "cannot request audio focus unless it's the top app or running a foreground service." [Manage audio focus](https://developer.android.com/media/optimize/audio-focus)
- **Automatic ducking (Android 8+):** when another app takes `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK` (TalkBack, navigation, an audible notification), the system ducks "all the active players of the first app" if it holds focus, is not playing `CONTENT_TYPE_SPEECH` and did not set `setWillPauseWhenDucked(true)`; "the first app is not notified". Ducking "isn't useful when playing spoken content … the app should pause instead." [Manage audio focus](https://developer.android.com/media/optimize/audio-focus) Both conditions assume the ducked app **holds** focus; the page says nothing about apps that never requested it.
- **Ducking others is for short audio:** `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK` is for when "you expect to play audio for only a short time … particularly suitable for apps that use the audio stream intermittently, such as for audible driving directions." [Manage audio focus](https://developer.android.com/media/optimize/audio-focus)
- **Attributes:** "Use `AudioAttributes` to describe the type of audio … for apps that play speech, specify `CONTENT_TYPE_SPEECH`." Use the same attributes in the focus request and the player. `AudioAttributes.DEFAULT` (media3) is `USAGE_MEDIA`, `CONTENT_TYPE_UNKNOWN`. [media3 AudioAttributes](https://developer.android.com/reference/androidx/media3/common/AudioAttributes)
- **No silent switch for media.** Ringer modes are "silent and will not vibrate" / "silent and will vibrate" / "may be audible and may vibrate"; the `AudioManager` reference ties ringer-mode behaviour to the ring stream by default. [AudioManager](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/AudioManager) Do Not Disturb's Alarms-only and Priority-only modes "won't mute sounds from music, videos, games, or other media"; only legacy Total silence did, and Android 14+ Modes let the person block or allow media explicitly. [Limit interruptions with Do Not Disturb](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9069335)
- **Accessibility guidance for media:** "Include controls that allow users to pause or stop the media, change the volume, and toggle subtitles." [Principles for improving app accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles)

### What `expo-audio` does with it

Read from [`AudioModule.kt`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/audio/AudioModule.kt), [`AudioRecords.kt`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/audio/AudioRecords.kt), [`AudioPlayer.kt`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/audio/AudioPlayer.kt).

- **Mode → focus gain:** `doNotMixPersistent` → `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN`; `doNotMix` → `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT`; `duckOthers` → `AUDIOFOCUS_GAIN_TRANSIENT_MAY_DUCK`; `mixWithOthers` → **no focus request at all**. The docs spell out the consequence: in `mixWithOthers` "no audio focus is requested, so focus-loss callbacks such as during phone calls are not received." [AudioMode](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/audio/#audiomode)
- **Focus is requested in `play()`** (`playWithAudioFocus`), after the silent-mode check; `FAILED` means no playback (58.0.0: "Playback no longer starts when the system denies audio focus"), `DELAYED` waits for the gain callback. The request uses `CONTENT_TYPE_MUSIC` and `setAcceptsDelayedFocusGain(true)`; the ExoPlayer itself is built with `setAudioAttributes(AudioAttributes.DEFAULT, false)` (focus handled by the module, not ExoPlayer) and `setHandleAudioBecomingNoisy(true)` (pause when headphones unplug). The focus request's attributes (`CONTENT_TYPE_MUSIC`) and the player's (`CONTENT_TYPE_UNKNOWN`) differ, against Android's advice; harmless for us, since neither is speech.
- **On focus loss:** `AUDIOFOCUS_LOSS` releases focus and pauses with no auto-resume; `LOSS_TRANSIENT` pauses and resumes on `GAIN`; `LOSS_TRANSIENT_CAN_DUCK` halves the volume only in `duckOthers` mode, otherwise pauses.
- **`playsInSilentMode: false`** on Android means `ringerMode == RINGER_MODE_NORMAL` is required to play; a ringer-mode receiver pauses playing players when the ringer goes silent or vibrate. There is no Do Not Disturb check. Added in 56.0.0.
- **Background:** with `shouldPlayInBackground: false` (default), going to the background releases focus and pauses; the foreground resumes what was interrupted. Without lock-screen controls, Android stops background audio "after approximately 3 minutes" anyway. [Playing audio in the background](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/audio/)

**What this means for a soundscape on Android:** there are two honest configurations, and `duckOthers` is not one of them (twenty minutes of ducking someone's music is what both platforms say not to do).

| Mode | Other apps' music | Calls and interruptions | TalkBack / navigation ducking |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mixWithOthers` | Keeps playing under ours | No callbacks; the system's call mute and Android 12 fade apply only to focus holders, so **test a call on the Pixel (unverified)**. Our own `AppState` → background pause covers the app being covered. | The automatic-ducking conditions name a focus holder, so **ducking may not reach us (unverified)**. Pause or duck ourselves when a screen reader runs (§8). |
| `doNotMix` (transient) or `doNotMixPersistent` | Paused while we hold focus, the whole Inquiry | Full callbacks; the system pauses us for calls and resumes transient losses | Ducked by the system, with no callback |

Android's convention is that media ignores the ringer, so `playsInSilentMode` should be `true` on Android even if it is `false` on iOS. The flag is one `Partial<AudioMode>`; `Platform.select` picks per platform.

## 3. Web: autoplay, background tabs and iPhone Safari

### What the platform imposes

- **A gesture first.** Media "will be allowed to autoplay only if at least one of the following is true": muted, "the user has interacted with the site (by clicking, tapping, pressing keys, etc.)", the site is allowlisted (Chrome's Media Engagement Index, or an installed PWA), or a Permissions-Policy grants it. Web Audio `start()` "outside the context of handling a user input event is subject to autoplay rules." `play()` rejects with `NotAllowedError`. [MDN: Autoplay guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Autoplay), [Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay)
- **A newly created `AudioContext` "will always begin in the `suspended` state"**, and the user agent may allow the transition to `running` "only when the AudioContext's relevant global object has sticky activation." [Web Audio API spec](https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/) MDN: "if you create the audio context from inside a click event the state should automatically be set to running … otherwise … it will need to be started after user interaction" with `resume()`. [Web Audio best practices](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Web_Audio_API/Best_practices) Safari on iOS: `play()` and `load()` "are also inactive until the user initiates playback, unless … triggered by user action … This requirement applies to media played by `<audio>` tags, `<video>` tags, and Web Audio." [Safari HTML5 audio guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/AudioVideo/Conceptual/Using_HTML5_Audio_Video/Device-SpecificConsiderations/Device-SpecificConsiderations.html) (archived)
- **Hidden tabs:** timers are throttled and `requestAnimationFrame` stops, but "tabs which are playing audio are considered foreground and aren't throttled." [MDN: Page Visibility API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Page_Visibility_API) Chrome's intensive throttling (one wake per minute after 5 minutes hidden) requires "the page has been silent for at least 30 seconds"; "a silent audio track doesn't count." [Chrome 88 timer throttling](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/timer-throttling-in-chrome-88) Chrome's lifecycle doc says pages observed "Playing audio" in the hidden state "won't be discarded unless under extreme resource constraints." [Page Lifecycle API](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/page-lifecycle-api) So a soundscape left running keeps the tab alive and un-throttled, which is a cost, not a feature, for a reading app.
- **iOS Safari interrupts.** `AudioContext.state` has an `interrupted` value: "interrupted by an occurrence outside the control of the web app", and on iOS Safari leaving the page ("switching tabs, minimizing the browser, or turning off the screen") moves the state to `interrupted`; the page must call `resume()` to continue. [MDN: BaseAudioContext.state](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/BaseAudioContext/state)
- **iPhone volume is the person's.** "On iOS devices, the audio level is always under the user's physical control. The `volume` property is not settable in JavaScript. Reading the `volume` property always returns 1." [Safari HTML5 audio guide](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/AudioVideo/Conceptual/Using_HTML5_Audio_Video/Device-SpecificConsiderations/Device-SpecificConsiderations.html) That is `HTMLMediaElement.volume`; a `GainNode` in Web Audio is a mix level, not the device volume, and is settable.
- **The silent switch on iPhone Safari (unverified):** a WebKit bug ("webaudio api is muted when the iOS ringer is muted", March 2022) and several developer write-ups report that Web Audio output is silenced by the ringer switch while `<audio>`/`<video>` keep playing, and that Safari's default web audio session is `ambient`. [WebKit bug 237322](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=237322) returned 403 to us; the mailing-list copy and the write-ups are secondary. The W3C **Audio Session API** (Working Draft, Media WG, 2024-11-13, edited by Apple and Mozilla) defines `navigator.audioSession.type` with `ambient` ("mixable with other types of audio"), `playback`, `transient`, `transient-solo` and `play-and-record`, and an `interrupted` state; a WebKit bug from 2024 says only Safari implements it, and only `type`. [Audio Session API](https://www.w3.org/TR/audio-session/), [WebKit bug 283417](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=283417) If the reports hold, Web Audio on iPhone already behaves like the native `.ambient` category we want.
- **Looping and fades in Web Audio:** `AudioBufferSourceNode.loop` with `loopStart`/`loopEnd`; "loop endpoints have subsample accuracy" and "playback of the loop is interpolated to splice the beginning and end of the loop together". [Web Audio API spec §1.9](https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/) `GainNode.gain.linearRampToValueAtTime` / `exponentialRampToValueAtTime` schedule fades; the exponential ramp cannot reach 0 ("the value needs to be positive"), so ramp to 0.01 or ramp linearly. [MDN: exponentialRampToValueAtTime](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/AudioParam/exponentialRampToValueAtTime) `HTMLMediaElement.loop` only "controls whether the media element should start over when it reaches the end"; no gaplessness is specified. [MDN: HTMLMediaElement.loop](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLMediaElement/loop)
- **WCAG 1.4.2 is binding on web** (and through WCAG2ICT on the app); see §7.

### What `expo-audio` does with it

Read from [`AudioPlayer.web.ts`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/src/AudioPlayer.web.ts), [`AudioModule.web.ts`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/src/AudioModule.web.ts), [`AudioUtils.web.ts`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/src/AudioUtils.web.ts).

- The player is `new Audio(uri)`: `loop` is `media.loop`, `volume` is `media.volume` (with a one-time warning on iOS Safari that it does nothing), `playbackRate` is the element's. No `AudioBufferSourceNode`, no `GainNode`, no ramps.
- `play()` catches the rejection; only `AbortError` is treated as benign, so a `NotAllowedError` surfaces as `playing: false` plus an `error` on the status (58.0.5 fixed the unhandled rejection "when the browser blocks playback before user interaction"). Nothing retries after a gesture; that is on us.
- `setAudioModeAsync` is `async function setAudioModeAsync(mode) {}`: a no-op. `setIsAudioActiveAsync(false)` pauses the players.
- A shared `AudioContext` is created lazily, only for audio sampling (`createMediaElementSource` + `AnalyserNode`), and is never `resume()`d.

**What this means on web:** `expo-audio` buys nothing we need here and loses two things we do (ducking on iPhone, gapless looping). We already own a Web Audio graph for dictation; a `soundscape.web.ts` with one `AudioContext` created in the first tap handler, `decodeAudioData` for the loop and the cues, an `AudioBufferSourceNode` with `loop = true` behind a `GainNode`, and `visibilitychange` → pause is small, and it is the same one-interface-per-platform pattern as `startDictation`.

## 4. Battery and thermal cost of a looping decoder

- **Android, from Google:** "For short audio playbacks or playbacks when the screen is on, audio does not have a significant impact on power. For long playbacks with the screen off, it's possible to save power by using ExoPlayer's audio offload mode", which moves decoding to a DSP and "limits the ability to apply audio effects". Video is dominated by "the display and decoding of the video stream". [ExoPlayer: battery consumption](https://developer.android.com/media/media3/exoplayer/battery-consumption) The soundscape is screen-on and foreground-only, so offload is irrelevant and the decoder is a rounding error next to the Skia world.
- **iOS, from Apple:** current documentation gives no audio power figures. The retired *Multimedia Programming Guide* said AAC, MP3, ALAC and HE-AAC had hardware-assisted decoding limited to one stream, with "linear PCM (uncompressed) or IMA4 (compressed)" for multiple simultaneous sounds, and that "software codecs … may entail significant CPU overhead." [Using Audio](https://developer.apple.com/library/archive/documentation/AudioVideo/Conceptual/MultimediaPG/UsingAudio/UsingAudio.html) (retired, Apple's own banner). A 2019 Core Audio mailing-list reply attributed to Apple says the hardware decoders are obsolete and the facilities deprecated **(unverified: the page returned 502; cited from the search summary)**. Treat decoding as CPU, and small.
- **Where the avoidable cost sits in `expo-audio`:**
  - **Status polling.** `updateInterval` defaults to 500 ms; on Android `BaseAudioPlayer` runs a coroutine `delay(updateInterval)` loop that emits `playbackStatusUpdate` to JS. Two JS events a second for twenty minutes, for a loop whose position we never read. Set it to tens of seconds, or rely only on `didJustFinish` for the cues. [AudioPlayerOptions](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/audio/#audioplayeroptions)
  - **Audio sampling.** On Android it is `android.media.audiofx.Visualizer`, which "requires `RECORD_AUDIO`". Never call `setAudioSamplingEnabled(true)`: it costs CPU and asks for the microphone permission for a decoration, against the closed list's spirit.
  - **The loop file.** Keep it short (a 30–60 s loop decoded from a file of a few hundred KB) and compressed; the decoder wakes per buffer either way. Mono is half the work and is what Mono Audio users hear anyway (§8).
- **Format.** Android decodes AAC-LC, MP3, Vorbis, Opus (5.0+), FLAC and PCM/WAVE on every version in scope. [Android supported media formats](https://developer.android.com/media/platform/supported-formats) iOS `AVPlayer` plays AAC in `.m4a`; browsers decode AAC/MP4 broadly, while Opus-in-Ogg is not decodable in Safari **(unverified for current Safari and for Firefox's AAC on Linux)**. AAC-LC in `.m4a` is the one file that plays on all three; for gapless looping see §5.
- **Thermal:** neither platform documents a thermal cost for audio; the Skia measurements ([#140](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/140)) are where a thermal budget would show, and the soundscape adds one decoder thread to that run. Hand #140 "the soundscape on" as a condition to measure rather than guessing.

## 5. Gapless looping and crossfading

| | iOS (`expo-audio`) | Android (`expo-audio`) | Web (`expo-audio`) | Web (own Web Audio) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-player `loop` | `AVQueuePlayer`, `actionAtItemEnd = .advance`, a copy of the item is enqueued (`enqueueNextLoopItem`); 56.0.0 "improved iOS looping", 58.0.9 re-applies the buffer duration on the loop item | `repeatMode == REPEAT_MODE_ONE` on ExoPlayer | `media.loop = true` | `AudioBufferSourceNode.loop`, "subsample accuracy" by spec |
| Playlist `loop: 'single'` | `seek(to: .zero)` then `play()` on `didPlayToEndTime`: a restart, not a loop | `REPEAT_MODE_ONE` | n/a | n/a |
| Documented gapless | Not claimed; Apple's `AVPlayerLooper` page (which `expo-audio` does not use) also never says "seamless" | Not claimed by `expo-audio`; ExoPlayer's gapless support depends on the format's gapless metadata **(unverified: the English docs page we read has no gapless section)** | Not specified by HTML | Yes, by spec |
| Fade / crossfade | None; `volume` is set directly on the `AVPlayer` | None; `setVolume` is immediate | None; `media.volume`, and not settable on iOS Safari | `GainNode` ramps (`linearRampToValueAtTime`, `exponentialRampToValueAtTime` to ≥ 0.01) |

Sources: [`AudioPlayer.swift`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/ios/AudioPlayer.swift), [`AudioPlaylist.swift`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/ios/AudioPlaylist.swift), [`AudioPlayer.kt`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/audio/AudioPlayer.kt), [`AudioPlaylist.kt`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/audio/AudioPlaylist.kt), [AVPlayerLooper](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/avfoundation/avplayerlooper), [Web Audio API spec](https://webaudio.github.io/web-audio-api/).

What follows for the build:

- **Use `player.loop`, not a playlist**, for the soundscape. The playlist's single-track loop on iOS is a hard restart.
- **Don't trust the loop point.** Compressed formats carry encoder delay and padding; whether `AVQueuePlayer` and ExoPlayer honour the container's gapless metadata for a looped item is not documented by either vendor or by `expo-audio`. The robust authoring fix is to make the file loop-safe regardless: a short loop whose tail is already crossfaded into its head, so a few milliseconds of gap or overlap at the seam is inaudible, and for the native build consider a lossless file (WAV/CAF PCM, or ALAC on iOS) for the loop since it is short **(unverified: audibility on the Pixel 8 Pro; a prototype can A/B `.m4a` against `.wav`)**.
- **Crossfades between places (forest → lake → landing) and fade-outs for Help and the Step-out** need two players and a JS-driven ramp on native: step `volume` every ~50 ms over the fade length from the sound module. Each step is a bridge call and an instant `AVPlayer.volume` / ExoPlayer `setVolume` write; coarse steps can zipper **(unverified: whether 20 steps over 1 s is audible on either platform)**. On web, `GainNode` ramps are sample-accurate and free. If native fades disappoint, the fallback is a small native module around `AVAudioEngine` / ExoPlayer's `AudioProcessor`, not a different JS library.
- **Cue sounds** are separate preloaded players (`createAudioPlayer` per cue, `downloadFirst` irrelevant for bundled assets), played over the loop; each is ≤ 2 s by the cue rule, so no fade is needed. `AVPlayer` is not a low-latency effects engine, and Apple's HIG says "use the system's sound services to play short sounds", which `expo-audio` does not bind **(unverified: cue onset latency through `AVPlayer`; measure in the prototype)**. [HIG: Playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio)

## 6. Ducking under "listen" and stopping for dictation

**"Listen" (TTS from `POST /speak`).** The TTS clip and the soundscape are both our players in one session, so "ducking" is arithmetic in the sound module, not a session option: lower the soundscape's level while the clip plays and restore it on the clip's `didJustFinish`. Two numbers from the standards set the target: WCAG **1.4.7 Low or No Background Audio (AAA)** asks background sounds to be "at least 20 decibels lower than the foreground speech content" ("roughly four times quieter"), or off, or switchable off, for prerecorded speech, so that "a user who is hard of hearing can separate the speech" [Understanding 1.4.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/low-or-no-background-audio.html); −20 dB is a linear gain of 0.1. It is AAA and about prerecorded media, so it is a design target, not a conformance line. On iPhone Safari the level is not settable through a media element, so **under web-iOS the duck is a pause** unless the soundscape runs on Web Audio (§3). The cross-app question (should "listen" duck *other apps'* music) is a different switch, `duckOthers` / `interruptSpokenAudioAndMixWithOthers` on iOS, and belongs to the "listen" ticket, not here.

**Dictation.** The sheet already says starting dictation stops playback; the platforms make that mandatory in different ways:

- **iOS:** `AudioStream.start()` runs `session.setCategory(.record, mode: .measurement)` and `setActive(true)`; `.record` is "recording audio while also silencing playback audio". `stop()` tears down the engine and **does not restore the category or deactivate**. [`AudioStream.swift`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/ios/AudioStream.swift) So the soundscape's output is cut the moment the stream starts whatever we do, and after the stream stops nothing plays until the sound module calls `setAudioModeAsync` again. Pause the soundscape *before* `stream.start()` so no player is left "playing" into a dead session, and re-apply the mode and resume *after* `stream.stop()`.
- **Android:** the stream opens `AudioRecord` and does not touch focus or the players ([`AudioStream.kt`](https://github.com/expo/expo/blob/sdk-58/packages/expo-audio/android/src/main/java/expo/modules/audio/AudioStream.kt), read for [#45][45]). Left alone, the soundscape keeps playing out of the speaker into the microphone, and the stream requests no voice processing, so Cloud STT hears birds. Pause is on us.
- **Web:** our `AudioWorklet` capture and a Web Audio soundscape share nothing automatic; `getUserMedia` with `echoCancellation: false` (as planned for STT fidelity) means the speaker feed is not cancelled. Pause is on us.
- The previous research's plan to set `interruptionMode: 'doNotMix'` while dictating stands; it also pauses other apps' music for the dictation's length, which is what both platforms expect of a recording app.

**One owner.** Three things now contend for one audio mode: the soundscape, "listen" and dictation. The sheet's rule for the session module (only it holds the token; every call goes through its client) fits here: one sound module owns `setAudioModeAsync`, the players and the stream's start/stop, and the lint that bans `Date.now` outside the composition root can ban `expo-audio` imports outside it.

## 7. Accessibility guidance on ambient sound in a reading app

- **WCAG 2.2, 1.4.2 Audio Control (Level A):** "If any audio on a Web page plays automatically for more than 3 seconds, either a mechanism is available to pause or stop the audio, or a mechanism is available to control audio volume independently from the overall system volume level." Intent: "individuals who use screen reading software can find it hard to hear the speech output if there is other audio playing at the same time", so they must be able to turn background sound off. "Muting the system volume is not 'pausing or stopping' the autoplay audio." The volume alternative must reach zero. The criterion must be met by all content on the page, "since any content that does not meet this success criterion can interfere with a user's ability to use the whole page." Sufficient techniques: **G60** a sound that stops within three seconds, **G170** a control near the beginning that turns off automatic sound, **G171** playing sounds only on user request. [Understanding 1.4.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html) WCAG2ICT carries it to software word for word with "whole non-web document or software". [WCAG2ICT](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag2ict-22/) Consequences: cue sounds ≤ 2 s are G60 by construction; a soundscape that only plays because the person turned a setting on is G171; a soundscape that plays by default needs a stop control that a screen-reader user reaches before the sound has got in the way, which the Settings boathouse at home is not when the sound plays inside a Path.
- **WCAG 2.2, 1.4.7 (AAA):** the −20 dB rule for speech over background (§6).
- **Apple HIG, Accessibility:** "Let people control audio and video playback. Avoid autoplaying audio and video content without also providing controls to start and stop it. Make sure these controls are discoverable and easy to act upon, and consider global settings that let people opt out of auto-playing all audio and video." "Use haptics in addition to audio cues … for people who can't perceive the audio or have their audio turned off." "It's important that dialogue and crucial information about your app or game isn't communicated through audio alone." [HIG: Accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) The HIG's "Playing audio" rules in §1 (silent mode, system volume, headphones, don't stop other apps' music) apply as written.
- **Android:** "Include controls that allow users to pause or stop the media, change the volume." [Principles for improving app accessibility](https://developer.android.com/guide/topics/ui/accessibility/principles) Material's sound guidance (Material 2) was not readable from its page; nothing cited from it.
- **Our own rules already cover the rest:** cues "never reward or count anything" and carry no meaning, so a cue sound is decoration and never the only channel for information (the HIG's "audio alone" rule is met by construction); the scene is hidden from screen readers, and a soundscape would be too.

## What a setting for it must control

Independent of #212's answer on *whether*, a setting for a soundscape has to control these, or the platform and WCAG rules above are broken:

1. **On or off, and the default.** WCAG 1.4.2 is met by construction when the sound plays only because the person turned it on (G171). A default-on soundscape needs instead a stop control reachable near the start of every place it plays, which the Settings boathouse is not from inside a Path. The HIG asks for "global settings that let people opt out"; a device-wide kept setting is that. The kept setting goes in the closed-list package and the settings store's record types, like Still water.
2. **Reachability.** The control must be reachable by a screen reader and from where the sound plays, named in glossary words, never by metaphor. If the only control is at home, the soundscape should not start inside an Inquiry on its own.
3. **Its own level, relative to the words.** "Adjust relative, independent volume levels … the system volume always governs the final output" (HIG). WCAG's alternative to a stop is a volume control that is independent of the system's and can reach zero. A fixed quiet level with a stop satisfies both; a slider is optional. The duck level under "listen" (≈ −20 dB) is a constant of the sound module, not a setting.
4. **What it never changes:** the system volume, the ringer, other apps' audio (no `duckOthers` for the bed), and the Reduce Motion / Still water behaviour of the world.
5. **Platform behaviour bundled with "on":** obeys the Ring/Silent switch and screen lock on iOS; ignores the ringer on Android; needs a tap on web; stops in the background and in a hidden tab; pauses for calls, headphones out and dictation; ducks (or pauses, on iPhone web media elements) under "listen"; sits under VoiceOver and TalkBack ducking.
6. **What it hands to #212:** whether cue sounds share the switch or have their own (G60 makes them WCAG-neutral; the HIG wants haptics beside any cue that carries meaning, and ours carry none); what Still water, Help and the Step-out do to the bed (fade out, hold, or nothing); and whether a screen reader running turns the default off.
