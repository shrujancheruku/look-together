# How Krishnamurti drew out and examined what people brought him

Research for [#2](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/2) (map: [#1](https://github.com/shrujancheruku/look-together/issues/1)).

**Question:** In his public talks and dialogues, what moves did Krishnamurti keep using to draw out a questioner's underlying Assumptions and examine them without judging? How did he respond when an exchange stalled? Which word did he use most for what people brought him, and in what sense?

## Answer in brief

- **Moves.** He rarely answered. He (1) questioned the question and the word in it, asking "what do you mean by that word?" and "what is a problem?"; (2) pointed out the premise or the claimed conclusion inside a question; (3) turned it back on the person ("it's your life, not my life", "find out, not from me"); (4) separated fact from idea, and "what is" from "what should be"; (5) asked the person to look at the thing without condemning, justifying or naming it; (6) presented the inquiry as shared and not his authority ("together we are going to find out").
- **Stalls.** He named the stall plainly ("we have spent fifty minutes and we haven't come to any point"), pulled the talk back to the thread ("I am asking you something and you are answering something else"), slowed it down ("Go slow"), had the group pick one question to work through completely, offered an analogy, and, when he had no answer, said "I do not know. I am asking" and left the question open.
- **Word.** In the dialogues, **"question"** is by far the more frequent word for what is put to him, and he treated the question as more important than any answer. **"Problem"** is his word for the concern a person actually lives with. He defined it as "something thrown at you, a challenge, something you have got to look at, face", meaning something to understand rather than to solve: "the answer is in the problem, not away from the problem." This *confirms* **Problem** as the glossary term, provided it means "something you have to face", not "something to fix". See [Implications](#implications-for-look-together).

## Recurring moves for drawing out Assumptions

### 1. Question the question, and the words in it

He made the question itself the first thing to examine.

- "Suppose I have a problem, first I question what is a problem." (S6)
- "Why has it become a repetitive question? Is our brain repetitive?" (S6)
- "Now what do you mean by that word 'intellectually'? Verbally?" (S2)
- "So the first thing is to find out what we mean by that word action, the doing." (S3)
- "Now what is a fact? What do you think is a fact?" and "Now what does that awareness mean?" (S1)

### 2. Expose the premise and the conclusion packed into the question

- To someone asking him for answers: "First of all if one may ask most respectfully, from whom do you expect the answers?" (S1)
- "Why do you ask me that question?" (S1)
- A questioner said "Action must be a creation, I feel." K: "You see, you are now making a statement like 'action must be creation' – we are finished; there is no dialogue." (S3) A stated conclusion ends the inquiry, so he treats it as something to open up.
- "Is it possible for an action not to have a cause?" K: "you are asking a question which is hypothetical, and the answer must also be hypothetical." (S3)
- "Sir, the gentleman says, 'I believe in myself' – so what is the thing you believe in?" (S3)
- He reversed a premise and then asked to look at it together. When A said the brain would be the instrument of the intellect, K replied: "But is not the intellect the instrument of the brain rather than the other way?" A: "Is it?" K: "Let us investigate it." (S4)

### 3. Hand it back: the person finds out, not the speaker

- "Sir, forget what I mean anything, because it's your life, not my life." (S2)
- "Think, watch it, sir, find out, not from me, find out, we will help each other." (S2)
- "It is not that the speaker is going to answer, like a politician," and "here we are together enquiring into these questions." (S1)
- "I am not investigating and you are just listening; together we are going to find out" (S3)
- "Am I talking sense or nonsense? You judge – please, you question what I am saying." (S1)
- "I am not saying you must accept it. I say I doubt it. I am not sure." (S3)

### 4. Separate fact from idea, "what is" from "what should be"

- "Is that true, a fact? We don't know." (S1)
- "We miss the whole thing because we confuse the word with the thing." (S4)
- "The 'what should be' is the opposite of 'what is' and 'what should be' is shaped by 'what is'." and "the ideal is the cause of the contradiction and therefore of the conflict." (S8)
- "the description is never the described, nor the word the thing." (S8)

### 5. Look without condemning, justifying or naming

This is his own version of "never judge an Assumption", applied to what is observed.

- "So can you observe that feeling without calling it violence?" (S2)
- "This requires tremendous observation, not discipline, just observation." (S2)
- "Just look at it, sir, look at it, examine it." (S3)
- "an awareness in which there is neither condemnation nor justification." (S7)
- "If I am condemning it or justifying it, surely there is no attention; I'm merely avoiding it." (S7)
- "I am not saying you shouldn't. It's up to you." (S2)

### 6. Stay with the problem rather than reach for an answer

- "the answer is in the problem, not away from the problem." (S7)
- "try to look at the problem without any fear or anxiety, and not look to an answer" (S7)
- "merely look at the problem, then the problem will begin to tell you." (S7)
- "So we are saying that in the very questioning is the answer." and "The question is far more important, is it not, than the answer." (S1)
- "The answer is not, if one may point out, separate from the question," (S1)

## When an exchange stalled

- **He named the stall.** "You know, we have spent fifty minutes and we haven't come to any point." (S3)
- **He pulled it back to the thread.** "I am asking you something and you are answering something else." (S2) "Now we are off to something… Please, sir, we are not talking who is enlightened…" (S3)
- **He slowed down and stopped premature closure.** "Go slow. Do not put it that way." (S4) "Do not say it cannot know." (S4)
- **He narrowed to one question.** "Now, which of these questions would you like to talk about?" and "take one and work it out completely together" (S3)
- **He asked for the point behind an image.** "This is fairly simple. What are you trying to say?" (S4)
- **He offered an analogy.** "It is like throwing a stone which is independent of the hand which throws it." (S4)
- **He let the question go back to the floor, then returned.** "Finish all your… and then let's come back." (S2) When asked "Could we go back to action?", he replied "I am coming… (laughs)." (S3)
- **He admitted not knowing and left it open.** "I do not know. I am asking." (S4) He also asked "Is there freedom at all?" and did not settle it: "I really do not know." (S4) One KFT compilation of his Q&A meetings is titled "Only the Question Remains" (Ojai 1984, first Q&A meeting; S9, title only).

He also pushed back hard, and that is a tone the app should not copy: "You haven't even listened, sir." (S2) "Apparently you have never asked that question of yourself." (S2)

## "Problem", "question" or something else?

- **"Question"** is what people put to him in the dialogues and Q&A meetings. In S1, "question" appears throughout while "problem" appears only twice. He gave the question priority over the answer (S1) and gave his Q&A meetings titles like "Is the Answer in the Question?" and "It Is Important To Put the Right Question" (S9, titles only).
- **"Problem"** is what a person carries in daily life. In S2 he pressed a questioner: "Is that your problem, sir?" Q: "Yes, that is my problem. It is the lack of love." K: "No, *your* problem." He separated the abstract issue from the one the person actually lives with.
- **His definition of "problem":** "According to the dictionary it means something thrown at you, a challenge, something you have got to look at, face." He added: "You can't dodge it, you can't run away from it, you can't suppress it." (S5) In S6 he called it "the very etymological meaning of that word": "something thrown or something that challenges you."
- **He also doubted problem-solving itself:** "Our brain is conditioned in problems. It is never free from problems." and "Can the brain ever be free of problems first, and then it can solve problems." (S5)
- "Conflict" is the word he used for the *result* of the gap between "what is" and "what should be" (S8). It names what the examination reveals, not what a person brings in.

## Implications for look-together

1. **Problem is confirmed, with a sense attached.** Krishnamurti's own sense is "something you have to face and look at", where the answer is found *in* it rather than produced as a fix. The glossary definition should say that, and the app shouldn't promise solutions. "Question" was his word for what was put to him, so it fits the Open Question the app leaves behind ("only the question remains").
2. **Breakdown moves to encode.** Ask what a key word means. Name the premise and any stated conclusion as Assumptions. Separate fact from idea and "what is" from "what should be". Hand the looking back to the person.
3. **Getting unstuck, in order.** Name the stall, return to the one thread, slow down, offer an analogy or a reversed premise, and otherwise say "I don't know" and leave an Open Question. This matches ADR 0001's "stuck → another angle → Open Question".
4. **Where Krishnamurti and ADR 0001 differ.** He sometimes did pronounce on things ("I am an Indian – that is an illusion" (S1); "It is all immature" (S4)). ADR 0001 is stricter than he was, and that is a reasonable choice for an app. It is a difference to know about, not a conflict. His non-judging is "neither condemnation nor justification" of what is observed, which is the same discipline.
5. **Concision.** His moves are short questions ("Why?", "Which means what?", "Is that a fact?"), so they suit the concise, direct voice even though his talks were long. His bluntness ("You haven't even listened, sir") would not suit newcomers.
